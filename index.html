See the data: why the raw ranking misleads

A borough's raw median depends on what its residents complain about. Police close requests fast, so a borough where most requests go to police looks fast; a borough with many housing and building requests looks slow. The adjusted index removes that effect by comparing each borough with the city on the same complaint types: 1.0 means typical, above 1.0 slower, below 1.0 faster. The last column redoes the index with instant closures included. Staten Island moves a lot, so its position is uncertain.

How to read this

  • Closed is not fixed. The data records when an agency closed a request, not whether the problem was solved.
  • Cleaning. — records were removed because they closed before they were created or after the data was pulled. — requests with no close date were kept and counted as not done.
  • Same-second closures. — requests were created and closed in the same second. They cluster in certain complaint types and boroughs and look administrative, so the borough comparison leaves them out. The agency and complaint speed figures keep them, which makes buildings, health, transportation and environmental protection look somewhat faster than they otherwise would. The speed table under the agency chart shows both versions.
  • Fair comparison. Speed measures use requests with at least three months to close. The backlog uses requests with at least six months.
  • Typical wait covers closed requests only. Where most requests never close, such as helicopter noise, it describes only the few that did. The share not done in a week counts every request.
  • Waiting totals are a floor. Time spent waiting counts closed requests only, so true waiting is longer.
  • Staten Island is uncertain. Its adjusted index moves a lot depending on how same-second closures are handled.
  • Surges are a correlation. A busy day and a long wait go together for some complaint types, not for others, and run the opposite way for a few. This analysis cannot show that one causes the other.

Source: NYC Open Data, 311 Service Requests from 2010 to Present (dataset erm2-nwe9), pulled on 2026-10-26. Population: US Census Bureau, Vintage 2025 estimates for July 1, 2025.
